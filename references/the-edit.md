# The edit — grammar, rhythm, retention

*Merged from: nateherkai/hyperframes-student-kit (video-storytelling, short-form-edit), kurbaitaev/ghost-editor (take selection, calibration data), and standard editing practice. Re-authored for this skill.*

This is the 70%: why cuts work, when to cut, and what a professional edit is doing that an AI edit usually isn't. Tools execute this file; they never replace it.

## 1 · Why the cut works at all

The brain constructs continuity across cuts — it completes the world off-screen (Kuleshov effect: meaning comes from juxtaposition, not from any single shot). This has two consequences for an editor:

- **The cut is a sentence, not an accident.** Placing shot B after shot A asserts a relationship: cause, contrast, answer, escalation. If no relationship is asserted, the viewer's brain invents one anyway — usually "this is random".
- **Continuity is a promise.** Everything the viewer has already seen still exists where they left it. Graphics worlds, diagram positions, and speaker framing obey this; resetting the world every few seconds is why "AI-made" videos feel confusing rather than defective — no single frame is wrong, and the feedback comes back vague.

## 2 · Murch's priority order

When deciding if a cut is right, weigh in this order (Walter Murch, *In the Blink of an Eye*):

| Priority | Weight | Question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Emotion | 51% | Does the cut preserve / advance the emotional beat? |
| Story | 23% | Does it move the information forward? |
| Rhythm | 10% | Is the timing right — not a beat early, not late? |
| Eye-trace | 7% | Is the viewer's eye already looking where the next shot begins? |
| 2D plane | 5% | Does screen direction / composition stay coherent? |
| 3D space | 4% | Is spatial continuity maintained (axis of action)? |

**Cut on emotion.** If a take is technically clean but emotionally dead, and a flubbier take is alive, the alive one wins. Rhythm sacrifices itself to emotion; never the reverse.

## 3 · The cut vocabulary

| Cut | Mechanics | Use it for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hard cut** | instant change | the default; 9 of 10 cuts in a calibrated reel are hard cuts |
| **J-cut** | audio of next shot leads by 0.5–2 s | scene transitions; pulling the viewer forward |
| **L-cut** | audio of current shot trails over next | interviews; letting a thought land over B-roll |
| **Match cut** | visual rhyme across the cut (shape, motion, idea) | topic pivots, chapter transitions |
| **Jump cut** | cut within the same shot (time elided) | vlog energy, compression — must be masked or owned |
| **Smash cut** | abrupt cut for contrast (loud→quiet, fast→still) | comedy beats, shock, punchlines |
| **Cross-cut** | alternating between parallel lines | tension, "meanwhile" |
| **Cutaway** | away to related imagery | covering edits, hiding jump cuts, breathing room |
| **Insert** | closer detail of the same space | emphasis, information (a number, a hand, a screen) |
| **Montage** | compressed sequence, motivated by music | time passage, process, escalation |
| **Invisible cut** | masked by whip pan, object pass, or wipe | seamless single-take illusion |
| **Freeze** | hold on a frame (0.3–1 s) | punchline landing, POV montage moments |

Calibration from shipped reels: **113 of 119 transitions were hard cuts; median shot ~3 s**. Dissolves and zooms are seasoning — a 0.2 s dissolve hides a jump cut, a punch-in lands a joke. Per-cut zooms annoy viewers in quantity; cap comic punch-ins at ~3 per short reel.

## 4 · Continuity grammar

- **180° rule** — keep the camera on one side of the axis of action so screen direction doesn't flip between shots of the same conversation.
- **30° rule** — vary the angle at least 30° between same-subject cuts, or the cut reads as a glitch rather than a new shot.
- **Eyeline match** — a look off-screen is a promise; the next shot pays it off from roughly where they looked.
- **Cut on action** — mid-movement cuts are masked by the motion itself; cutting on stillness exposes the edit.
- **Enter the way the eye exits** — if content exits frame-right, the next shot's subject enters from the left region. This is eye-trace made mechanical.

## 5 · Timing the cut

- **Blinks and cuts are the same event.** Viewers blink when their internal picture changes; cut points and blink points coincide (Murch again).
- **Breath is the talking-head metronome.** Cut on the breath between phrases, never mid-inhale.
- **Word onsets are the graphics clock.** Any overlay, card, or highlight enters 0.15–0.25 s *ahead* of its anchor word and never divides evenly — "time everything to word starts, never to arithmetic".
- **Land the audio with the visual** — an impact SFX aligns its audible onset (not the file start; files have leading silence) within 2 frames of the visual landing.

## 6 · Pacing by format

| Format | Median shot | Rhythm shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Long-form talking-head (YouTube) | 4–8 s on A-roll | B-roll every 15–40 s; pattern interrupt every 20–30 s; 4–6 s outro |
| Tutorial / screencast | cut on UI events | cuts follow actions, not time |
| Vertical short (Reels / Shorts / TikTok) | 1–3 s | burst → anticipation → payoff; escalate and release |
| Documentary / narrative | 2–6 s, shot-length curves accelerate toward climax | tension shortens shots; release lengthens them |

Treat "a thing happens every 1–2 s" in shorts as a **diagnostic, not a metronome**. When action or performance earns a longer hold, hold — and know why. Escalate and release: a run of tight discoveries, a brief anticipation, then the payoff, with short stillness to land conviction. Silence in the sound bed makes the next card hit.

## 7 · Retention mechanics

**The first 3 seconds are a separate edit.** The gate is one question: *is someone convinced they need to watch until the end?* Build the opening as its own piece — hook, unresolved question, visible evidence by second three, and the exact payoff scene already chosen. The opening must work muted. Reject vague teasing and any promise the footage cannot fulfill. Vary the device between videos; a repeated opening stamp reads as a template, not an edit.

**Open loops are spatial, not verbal.** Never say "we'll come back to this" — leave something visibly incomplete on screen and return to it. The mechanic: plant in the hook → re-show at every section boundary (~2 s each) → advance it visibly → close on the image you opened on. Pick the shape from the script:

| Script shape | The world shows | What is incomplete |
| --- | --- | --- |
| N parallel items | N sockets on a spine | empty slots |
| One process, deep | the full pipeline laid out | a broken / missing link |
| Levels or tiers | a stack building upward | the ghost top of the stack |
| Comparison | two columns on one axis | one column empty |
| News / announcement | a timeline or landscape map | the right side — the future — is blank |
| Tutorial / build-along | the finished architecture, ghosted | every node not yet built |

**Pattern interrupts** hold attention across a long runtime: a framing change, a number rolling, a topic card, a B-roll arrival — one roughly every 20–30 s in talking-head long-form, denser in shorts.

**Loop-crafting (vertical only):** the last frame should flow back into the first — match end state to start state, or cut on an action completing so the restart reads as rhythm, not a glitch.

## 8 · Attention is a spotlight

**Exactly one thing at full brightness; everything else stays quiet but legible.** Hierarchy comes from the *ratio* between active and quiet, never from dimming everything. If the viewer cannot tell signal from texture, "too much going on" is what they'll say — and they'll be right.

- **One thing to follow.** Carry the story on a single travelling subject whose state visibly changes; it never teleports, it moves along drawn connectors.
- **If it doesn't get a word, it doesn't get drawn.** Every element is either labelled, a connector between labelled things, the travelling subject, or ambient ground. Budget ~20 on-screen words per section; labels are 1–2 words.
- **Never overlay the anti-pattern on the recommendation.** Take the world near-black first, or place the counter-example where the good diagram isn't.

## 9 · The talking-head edit, specifically

- **Choose takes from the transcript table, never by scrubbing.** One clean take = autocut; messy takes = pick per sentence.
- **Cut on word times**: in at `first word − 0.10..0.15 s`, out at `last word + 0.20..0.30 s`. When a meme or stinger follows a line, let the take run 0.6–1 s past the last word so the sound has air.
- **Whisper merges restarts.** False starts and re-spoken phrases collapse into one clean-looking sentence with smeared timestamps. Re-transcribe the rendered rough cut and audit it verbatim — a long stretch of voice carrying few transcribed words is a hidden retake.
- **Mask jump cuts** with a 0.2 s dissolve, a shot-size change, or a B-roll insert — not with a zoom on every cut.
- **Leave air in the cut.** A gap ~10× tighter than the speaker's median sentence gap is heard as a stutter. Repair with 4–8 frames of real room tone (10 ms fades in, 40 ms equal-power crossfade at the tails), never digital silence.
- **A-roll / B-roll interleave**: B-roll carries the example, evidence, or consequence — never a third restatement of the narration. A captioned still with a camera move is a photographic layer, not moving B-roll. Use each distinct B-roll scene only once per deliverable.
- **Presence**: ~40% speaker-on-screen across a graphics-led video reads right; long absences are fine at payoff beats — audit them, don't cap them.

## 10 · The editing-slop death list

The default failure modes of AI-edited video. Each line has shipped in the wild:

1. Cutting only on sentence boundaries — metronome rhythm, no judgment.
2. Wall-to-wall zooms / punch-ins on every cut.
3. Every transition is a crossfade (or worse, a wipe from a preset pack).
4. Captions that cover the speaker's face, or sit under the platform UI.
5. Music that never ducks under speech; "voice vs bed" settled by loudness war.
6. Silence removal crushed to zero-gap — digital dropouts heard as stutters.
7. A new full-screen graphic every ~3 s, resetting the world (scene-thinking).
8. Logo bumper before any value; 0.3 s of brand before second zero earns a swipe.
9. SFX whoosh on every cut; impacts that land 5 frames after the visual.
10. Center-cropping a two-person interview to 9:16 — the window lands between the faces.
11. Shipping without captions, or with captions nobody frame-checked for the longest line.
12. One "AI intro" stamp device reused on every video the agent touches.
13. Reusing the same B-roll scene twice because it "looked good".
14. Claiming the edit is "verified" from code inspection — no frame was ever looked at.

The fix for every line is upstream in this file: cuts answer emotion/story/rhythm, one spotlight, open loops, word-timed everything, verify by looking.
